App Store Connect

Prerequisites

Complete these once before creating any products. If they're already done, skip ahead to Step 2.

1

Enroll in the Apple Developer Program

Go to developer.apple.com → Account → join the paid program ($99/year). In-app purchases are not available on free accounts.

2

Sign the Paid Applications Agreement

1. In App Store Connect, go to Agreements, Tax, and Banking (top menu)
2. Find "Paid Applications" — if status is "New" or "Action Required", click it
3. Review and accept the agreement
4. Status must show Active before products can go live
Do this first. Products created before the agreement is active will be stuck in "Missing Metadata" and won't appear in the app.
3

Add bank account and tax forms

In Agreements, Tax, and Banking:
• Banking → Add a bank account (routing + account number, or IBAN)
• Tax → Complete the US tax interview (W-9 for US entities, W-8BEN for non-US). Takes 5–10 minutes. Apple will not pay out until this is complete.
• Allow 1–3 business days for banking to verify
4

Confirm bundle ID capabilities

In developer.apple.com → Certificates, IDs & Profiles → Identifiers:
• Find com.apptist.Poplur2025
• Confirm In-App Purchase is checked (it's on by default, but verify)
App Store Connect

Creating the products

1

Open the In-App Purchases section

1. Go to appstoreconnect.apple.com
2. Click My Apps → select Poplur
3. In the left sidebar under "Features", click In-App Purchases
4. Click the + button to create a new product
2

Choose type: Consumable

In the dialog, select Consumable for all 6 products (3 credit packs + 3 radius boosts).
For the Daily Upgrade, select Non-Consumable.
Click Create.
3

Fill in product details — repeat for each product

Reference Name: Internal name (not shown to users). Use the display names from the table on page 1 — e.g. "Starter Pack", "City Boost".

Product ID: Copy exactly from the table — e.g. com.apptist.Poplur2025.credits.100. This cannot be changed after creation.

Pricing: Click "Set a Price" → choose the matching price tier (e.g. Tier 2 = $1.99, Tier 8 = $7.99, Tier 20 = $19.99 in most regions). Apple handles currency conversion automatically.

Localizations → Add English (US):
  • Display Name: matches the table (e.g. "Starter Pack")
  • Description: 1–2 sentences shown in the App Store. Example for Starter Pack: "100 AI Creator Credits for Poplur. Use for image, video, voice, and music generation. Credits never expire."

Review Notes: Add a short note for Apple's review team: "This is a consumable that adds AI generation credits to the user's Poplur account. Credits are debited each time the user generates AI content."
4

Add a screenshot

Apple requires a 640×920 px screenshot showing the feature the purchase unlocks. For credits, screenshot the Poplur AI screen. For radius, screenshot the map with a radius indicator. JPEG or PNG, no transparency.
5

Save and submit for review

Click Save. Status changes to "Ready to Submit".
IAPs are reviewed alongside the app binary — they don't need a separate submission. When you submit a new app version, all "Ready to Submit" IAPs are included automatically.
Tip: You can test IAPs in the simulator immediately after creation using StoreKit Testing (via Xcode's StoreKit configuration file) without waiting for Apple review. For device testing, use a Sandbox tester account.
App Store Connect

Sandbox testing

1

Create a Sandbox tester account

1. In App Store Connect, go to Users and Access → Sandbox → Testers
2. Click + and enter a new email address you control (must not be an existing Apple ID)
3. Fill in first name, last name, select country (use your primary App Store country)
4. Save — no email confirmation needed for Sandbox accounts
2

Sign into the sandbox on a physical device

1. On the test iPhone, go to Settings → App Store
2. Scroll to Sandbox Account → sign in with the tester email/password
3. Do NOT sign out of your real Apple ID — sandbox uses a separate field
4. Run the Poplur app in debug build and attempt a purchase
3

Verify the purchase flow

• Sandbox purchases show a banner "Environment: Sandbox"
• No real money is charged
• After a successful sandbox purchase, check Firestore Console → creatorCreditBalances → {uid} to confirm availableCredits incremented
• Also check users → {uid} → creditPurchases for the idempotency record
4

Test edge cases

• Purchase → kill app before delivery completes → relaunch → credits should be restored via StoreKitTransaction.unfinished
• Purchase the same pack twice → second delivery should be a no-op (idempotency key blocks duplicate credits)
• Check the AI chat → use all credits → verify 402 error → tap "Get Credits" → store opens
Simulator testing (no device needed): In Xcode, go to File → New → StoreKit Configuration File. Add the same product IDs. In the scheme editor, set the StoreKit config file. Purchases in the simulator will use this local config instead of the server, with instant approvals.
Google Play

Prerequisites

Android billing not yet implemented. The Android codebase currently uses mock purchase state. Before these steps matter, the Google Play Billing Library (v6+) must be integrated. See the implementation note at the end of this guide.
1

Enroll in the Google Play Developer Program

Go to play.google.com/console → pay the one-time $25 USD registration fee.
Identity verification is required — takes 1–3 business days.
2

Set up a Google Payments merchant account

1. In the Play Console, go to Setup → Payments profile
2. Click Create payments profile
3. Enter business name, address, and banking details
4. Submit tax information (W-9 or W-8BEN equivalent)
5. Allow 3–5 business days for verification
Without a verified payments profile, in-app products can be created but not purchased. Users will see "This item is not available for purchase."
3

Upload at least one APK or AAB to the console

Google requires an app binary before in-app products can be created. Upload a release or internal testing build via Production or Internal testing track. The binary does not need to be live — draft status is fine.
Google Play

Creating the products

1

Navigate to In-app products

1. In Play Console, select Poplur from your apps
2. In the left menu: Monetize → Products → In-app products
3. Click Create product
2

Create each product — repeat 7 times

Product ID: Use the same IDs as iOS (see page 1 table). Google uses the same format — e.g. com.apptist.Poplur2025.credits.100.

Name: Display name shown to users (matches iOS) — e.g. "Starter Pack"

Description: Same short description as App Store

Price: Click Set price → enter USD base price. Google auto-converts to 150+ currencies. Match the iOS pricing exactly (e.g. $1.99, $7.99, $19.99, $9.99, $19.99, $49.99, $0.99).

Status: Set to Active immediately (unlike iOS, no Apple-style review needed for IAPs — they go live when activated).

Click Save.
3

Note: Daily Upgrade is non-consumable on iOS but should be a subscription on Android

On Android, Google prefers subscriptions for ongoing access benefits. However, if it's a true one-time unlock (no recurring benefit), create it under In-app products (not Subscriptions) and it will behave as a non-consumable.
Product IDs are final. Google, like Apple, does not allow product IDs to be renamed or reused after deletion. Get them right the first time.
Google Play

Testing on Android

1

Add license testers

1. In Play Console, go to Setup → License testing
2. Add Gmail addresses of test accounts (your own email, team emails)
3. Set response to "RESPOND_NORMALLY"
License testers can make purchases without being charged — Google shows a fake card entry.
2

Add testers to a testing track

1. Go to Testing → Internal testing → add the tester Gmail accounts
2. Upload the debug APK/AAB
3. Testers must download the app from the Play Store test link (not sideloaded) for in-app purchases to work
3

Verify the purchase and delivery

• On the test device, sign in with the license tester Google account
• Open the app → tap a purchase → see the Google Play test purchase dialog
• After confirming, verify Firestore: creatorCreditBalances → {uid} → availableCredits incremented
• Check users → {uid} → creditPurchases for the delivery record
Android billing implementation required. The Android app needs the Google Play Billing Library (com.android.billingclient:billing-ktx:7.x) added to the project, plus a BillingClientManager / CreatorCreditBillingViewModel that mirrors the iOS CreatorCreditPurchaseViewModel pattern. The Firestore delivery logic is identical — use the same product IDs and write to the same creatorCreditBalances collection.
Final checklist

Before going live

App Store Connect

  • Paid Applications agreement status is Active
  • Bank account verified and tax forms complete
  • All 7 products created with correct product IDs (copy from page 1)
  • Each product has a screenshot and English localization
  • Sandbox purchase tested — credits appear in Firestore
  • Idempotency tested — same purchase twice only delivers credits once
  • Unfinished transaction restore tested — kill app mid-purchase, relaunch, credits delivered
  • AI chat 402 flow tested — "Get Credits" opens store, purchase resolves the error

Google Play Console

  • Payments merchant account verified
  • APK/AAB uploaded to at least one track
  • All 7 products created with matching product IDs and status Active
  • License testers added
  • Google Play Billing Library integrated in Android app (see below)
  • Android purchase tested end-to-end (credits appear in Firestore)
  • Idempotency tested on Android
Android

Implementation note

The Android app needs Google Play Billing Library wired up. The required Kotlin additions:

Add to app/build.gradle:
implementation "com.android.billingclient:billing-ktx:7.1.1"

Create a BillingClientManager.kt that wraps BillingClient, calls queryProductDetailsAsync, and launches launchBillingFlow.

On PurchasesUpdatedListener success, write to users/{uid}/creditPurchases/{orderId} and increment creatorCreditBalances/{uid} — identical to iOS logic.

Same IDs as iOS. Define as constants in a CreatorCreditProduct sealed class or companion object.

Revenue share: Apple takes 30% (15% for developers earning under $1M/year via the Small Business Program). Google also takes 30% (15% after 12 months of the same subscription, or the $1M threshold program). Factor this into your credit pricing — a $1.99 pack nets approximately $1.39 after fees.
